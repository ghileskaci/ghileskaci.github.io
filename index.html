01 / ABOUT

About me.

My work has taken me from field experiments and university teaching to satellite data and agricultural water research.

I am an agricultural scientist working at the intersection of agronomy, satellite remote sensing, and water resources.

As an Associate Research Scientist with Texas A&M AgriLife Research at the High Plains Research and Extension Center (HPREC) in Canyon, Texas, I study agricultural water use and how reliably we can measure it using satellite observations, field data, and physical models.

My background is rooted in field agronomy. During my doctoral research in Algeria and France, I studied wheat–faba bean intercropping, soil fertility, and plant nutrition, particularly the processes that influence nitrogen and phosphorus uptake. Working directly with crops, soils, and field experiments shaped the way I approach research today. It taught me that a convincing model is not necessarily an accurate one, and that observations remain essential for testing our assumptions.

My career has also included university teaching and international agricultural projects. At the University of Boumerdes, I taught plant physiology, soil biology, and experimental methods. I later worked on crop mapping and agricultural training initiatives in Mauritania and contributed to open-science education. These experiences strengthened my interest in research that is both scientifically rigorous and accessible to the people who use it.

Today, I bring that experience to the study of agricultural water use across fields and landscapes. Satellite observations provide an extraordinary view of agriculture, but they also have limitations. I am particularly interested in understanding those limitations, investigating disagreements between models and field measurements, and developing methods whose assumptions and uncertainties can be examined openly. For me, the value of a scientific result lies not only in what it shows, but in how confidently we can stand behind it.
